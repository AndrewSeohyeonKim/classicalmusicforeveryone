Outreach Concerts

Live classical music carried into the rooms people are already in — care homes, parishes, hospitals, hostels and community spaces.

Invite a concert → Other ways in

A room without a stage is still a venue.

A care home, a hospital, a hostel, a rural parish is not a place without an audience. It is where the audience that has waited longest already is.

We bring the players, the instruments, the stands and a programme chosen for that particular room. The room provides the room. From a homeless hostel in Dublin 7 to a martyrs’ shrine in Daegu, the arrangement has not changed.

How it works

What the room actually has to provide.

The most common reason a room says no is a belief that it needs equipment, a budget or a stage. It needs none of those. This is the entire list, on both sides.

Who it is for

  • Residential and nursing care, and day services.
  • Parishes, shrines, convents and retired religious communities.
  • Hospitals — atriums and day rooms as readily as chapels.
  • Hostels and homeless services.
  • Community centres, and any room where people are already gathered.

What happens

  1. An invitation, and a short conversation about who will be in the room.
  2. A programme built for that audience, not a touring set list.
  3. We arrive early, set up quietly, and work around the room’s routine.
  4. Thirty to sixty minutes, at a volume the room can hold.
  5. We stay afterwards. That part is often the reason we were asked.

What we do not claim

Audience numbers were never counted. We have photographs, programmes and a small number of institutional letters, and that is the whole of the evidence — we would rather say twenty performances and no audience figure than produce an estimate.

Next step

If you have a room, you have a venue.

Tell us the setting, roughly how many people, and a rough time of year. There is no charge to the audience, and we will be straight with you about cost.
