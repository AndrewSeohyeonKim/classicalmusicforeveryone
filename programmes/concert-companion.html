Concert Guide & Companion

Small groups — about five people — prepared beforehand, sat with during, and asked about afterwards.

Come on the next outing → Other ways in

The ticket is rarely the barrier.

What stops people is not knowing what happens when you get there, and having nobody to go with. Both of those are solvable, and neither costs money.

We prepare in advance, sit together, use the interval for questions, and talk about it afterwards. Tickets for the concerts we go to can be as little as £8, which surprises almost everybody.

How it works

An outing is three parts. The concert is the middle one.

Treat it as an evening out and the preparation and the conversation afterwards look like extras. They are not: they are the two parts that make the middle one possible for someone who would never go alone.

Who it is for

  • People who have wanted to go for years and never had anyone to go with.
  • People who worry about when to clap, what to wear, or where to sit.
  • Anyone new to Ireland who would like a first cultural outing in company.
  • Participants from the lecture series ready to hear it live.
  • Wheelchair users and people with limited mobility — we check access first.

What happens

  1. We agree a concert and get the tickets sorted well beforehand.
  2. A short preparation: what the piece is, and what the room will do.
  3. We meet before the doors and go in together.
  4. The interval is for questions, including the ones that feel too obvious.
  5. Afterwards we say what we heard. There is no wrong answer.

What we do not claim

This is the least well documented of the five. Invitations survive, attendance does not, and the ten outings were reconstructed from teaching slides rather than logged on the day. Outings from autumn 2026 are recorded properly.
