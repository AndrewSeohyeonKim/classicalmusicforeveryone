No entry requirement, and nothing to prepare.

An hour of talking and playing, built for people who like the sound of classical music and have never had anyone to ask about it.

The series runs in three stages with seasonal specials across them — European summer festivals, the BBC Proms, the Wexford Opera Festival, Christmas. Attendance has grown from six at the first session to fourteen.

How it works

Three stages that move ownership, not difficulty.

Nobody is being brought up to a standard. What moves across the three stages is whose music it is — and each session is built to stand on its own, so a first-timer never arrives having missed the beginning.

Who it is for

  • People who like the sound of it and have never had anyone to ask.
  • People who assume classical music is not for them, on grounds of education.
  • Anyone new to Ireland looking for a low-stakes room to be in.
  • Regulars who now come for the arguing as much as the music.
  • Nobody is tested, and no question is too basic to ask out loud.

What happens

  1. An hour, in a hall or a parish room. Chairs, a screen, an instrument.
  2. One idea, one composer or one question — not a survey of everything.
  3. Recorded excerpts, played loud enough to be worth hearing.
  4. Live performance in the room, close enough to see the fingering.
  5. Talking afterwards, for as long as anyone wants to stay.

What we do not claim

Attendance is counted per session, not per person, so 143 is attendances and not 143 individuals — a regular is counted each time they come. We have not asked participants what changed for them in any structured way.
